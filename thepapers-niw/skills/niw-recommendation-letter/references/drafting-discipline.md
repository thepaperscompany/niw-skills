# Drafting rules for anything that goes to USCIS

These apply to the endeavor statement, recommendation letters and the petition letter alike. A document filed with a federal agency is held against the petitioner if it overclaims, and a claim nobody can verify is worse than no claim.

## Contents

- Evidence fidelity
- Write for a skeptical reader
- Keep the prongs separate
- What never appears in a draft
- Self-critique beside the draft

## Evidence fidelity

**You cannot verify anything from memory, so do not write anything that depends on memory.**

- Reference a national priority, statute, regulation, executive order, agency program, funding opportunity, report, standard, or decision **only** through a source supplied to you. Do not name, cite, paraphrase or quote one that is not in front of you.
- **Place no text in quotation marks unless it is copied verbatim from a supplied source.**
- Do not invent report numbers, docket numbers, order numbers or program names.
- Cite only exhibit ids that appear verbatim in the manifest.
- Where supplied evidence is thin, write a more general framing that the endeavor advances, rather than inventing a specific authority. **A document citing fewer real sources is far stronger than one citing a fabricated or misremembered authority.**

## Write for a skeptical reader

The reader is an adjudicator looking for a reason to refuse, not an audience to be impressed.

- Confident where the record supports it, restrained where it does not.
- Specific over sweeping. "Deployed at four named hospital systems covering 220,000 annual encounters" beats "widely adopted".
- No adjectives doing an evidence's job: not "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "groundbreaking", "world-class".
- Every claim either points at a document or is visibly framed as a plan rather than an accomplishment.

## Keep the prongs separate

Prong 1 is about the **endeavor**: what it is, what it produces, who benefits beyond the petitioner's own employer, and how the benefit travels. Prong 2 is about the **person**: their documented record and why it positions them to advance this endeavor.

Using the petitioner's achievements to argue Prong 1 is a category error adjudicators penalize. Treat field-level importance as framing only, and never as proof of the endeavor's national importance.

## What never appears in a draft

- A prediction of approval, a promise of success, or any probability of success.
- An assertion the supplied record does not support.
- Em-dashes. Use a comma, colon, period or parentheses.
- Literal `\n` where a paragraph break belongs. Use real line breaks.
- A cover page, mailing address, signature block, exhibit index or certificate, unless the packet facts for those were supplied.

## Self-critique beside the draft

Deliver the draft with a short, honest list of its weakest points: which claims rest on the thinnest evidence, which sentence an adjudicator is most likely to challenge, and what document would close the gap.

A draft handed over without this reads as finished when it is not. The critique is what makes it usable.

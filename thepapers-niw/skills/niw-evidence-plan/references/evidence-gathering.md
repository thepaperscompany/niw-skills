## Two kinds of item, and when you may emit each

**Essential** items are true regardless of any assessment:

1. The standard filing documents every NIW needs: degree certificates and transcripts, CV, passport biographic page, current immigration status documents.
2. Documentation for what the record **already asserts**. If the profile lists "US Patent 11,234,567", ask for the grant certificate and any assignment or licensing record, by that number. If it names an employer, role or award, ask for the document proving it.

**Recommended to strengthen** items close a specific identified weakness.

**You may only emit the second kind when you have an assessment.** With a profile alone you know what the petitioner claims, and nothing about how those claims fare. Strengthening presupposes knowing what is weak. Inventing that assessment while building a list is the failure this rule exists to prevent. With a profile and no evaluation, emit essential items only, and say why the list is short.

An asserted but undocumented claim is the weakest thing in a petition, and the petitioner's own record tells you which claims exist.

## Ask for what the archetype actually produces

Do not ask a founder for citation reports, or an industry professional for editorial board invitations.

- **Academic researcher**: independent citation reports with self- and collaborator-citations separated out, evidence of methods or datasets adopted by other groups, funded-role documentation showing whether they were PI or co-PI, peer review and editorial service, conference invitations.
- **Industry professional**: named deployments with scale, external customers or users, licensing or adoption outside the employer, standards-body contributions, regulatory submissions, internal architecture or impact documents that name the petitioner's role.
- **Entrepreneur or founder**: executed pilot or customer agreements naming the counterparty, investment documentation, revenue records, job creation records, third-party validation of the specific plan rather than the market.
- **Hybrid**: cover each track the record genuinely supports. Do not average two vocabularies into a list that fits neither.

## Fields that do not produce the standard markers

Some fields have no peer-reviewed venues, no named prizes and no formal societies, and the petitioner is not weaker for it. Do not pad a list with items their field cannot generate, and do not treat their absence as a gap.

Ask instead for the artifacts that field does produce and that show what the standard markers would have shown: who adopted the work, who paid for it, who selected this person over alternatives, and who outside their own organization relies on it. Name the specific artifact for that field: a commissioned report, a regulatory filing, a production credit, a code of practice, a procurement award, a named deployment. Not a generic "evidence of recognition".

This is an evidence question about the merits. It is distinct from the comparable-evidence provision at 8 CFR 204.5(k)(3)(iii), which concerns qualifying for the EB-2 classification and applies only on the exceptional-ability path.

## Document the claims the record already makes

Prioritize evidence that moves a claim from unsupported or inferred toward documented, and that establishes either the endeavor's specific national importance or the petitioner's documented record of success: independent citations and adoption, named deployments or users, funded roles, third-party interest.

Field-level framing and credential recitation are not priorities. The current standard treats them as insufficient on their own, so a list padded with them is longer without being stronger.

## Naming sources without inventing them

When external public data would strengthen an item, name the authoritative source by its known name so the petitioner can find it: "the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics for your occupation".

**Never output a URL, a document identifier, an accession number, or a publication date you cannot verify.** Any such specific, produced from memory, is a fabrication. Describe each item by its kind and its source's name.

## Grouping

Group by document type, so the list maps onto how a petitioner actually files things: recommendation letters, publications, media coverage, government and industry reports, diplomas and degrees, awards and honors, patents, employment records, identity and status documents.

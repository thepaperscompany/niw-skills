## PA-2026-05: full discretion to deny without an RFE or NOID

**Alert:** PA-2026-05, "Evidence, Requests for Evidence, and Notices of Intent to Deny," dated August 4, 2026.
**Effective:** applies to requests pending or filed on or after August 5, 2026.
**Regulatory basis:** 8 CFR 103.2(b)(8), and specifically 8 CFR 103.2(b)(8)(ii).
**Verified:** 2026-09-01, against the policy alert published by USCIS.

The alert states that "USCIS has full discretion to deny the request without first issuing an RFE or NOID in various situations including, but not limited to, when the benefit request has no legal basis for approval or when the requestor fails to provide required initial evidence." Prior guidance discouraged officers from denying without first issuing an RFE or NOID where the record failed to establish eligibility or the requestor failed to submit required initial evidence. That discouragement is removed.

**What this changes about how you advise a petitioner.** A weak or incomplete filing can no longer be assumed to draw a curable Request for Evidence. The RFE was, in practice, a second chance to document what the record did not carry, and a petitioner who filed early on the theory that they could fix it later was making a bet that this alert has taken off the table.

Three consequences that must reach the petitioner in plain terms:

1. **Filing readiness is now the whole decision.** Where the analysis says a prong does not yet clear preponderance on documented evidence, "file and see" is no longer a low-cost option. Say so directly, and say why.
2. **Initial required evidence is a separate failure mode from a weak argument.** A petition that omits evidence required by statute, regulation or the form instructions can be denied on that basis alone, before anyone weighs the Dhanasar prongs.
3. **Do not soften a not-ready verdict.** The cost of a premature filing rose. The honest verdict is more valuable than it was, not less.

**What this does not change.** It does not change the Dhanasar framework, the preponderance standard, or what evidence establishes any prong. It changes the consequence of filing short, not the substance of the test. Do not describe it as a change in the legal standard, and never suggest it makes approval more or less likely for a given record.

## PA-2025-16: discretionary analysis after eligibility

Meeting the EB-2 classification and establishing all three Dhanasar prongs by a preponderance of the evidence makes a petitioner eligible for the national interest waiver. It does not guarantee approval. The waiver is also subject to a separate, favorable exercise of discretion assessed after threshold eligibility is met.

Among employment-based immigrant petitions, this discretionary determination applies specifically to national interest waiver cases. Ordinary employment-based petitions are eligibility-based; NIW layers a totality-of-the-circumstances discretionary analysis on top of the three-prong analysis. The analysis weighs the petitioner's overall circumstances, including conduct, character, immigration history, and compliance with immigration law, independent of how strong the endeavor and positioning are. A well-documented three-prong case can still be denied on discretion.

**How to apply it.** Keep eligibility and discretion analytically separate. Where the record shows an adverse factor relevant to discretion, such as an immigration-compliance issue, flag it as a distinct denial risk rather than as a prong deficiency. Never infer or invent conduct facts that are not in the record, and never let the discretionary layer dilute a rigorous three-prong assessment.

The waiver is discretionary in nature. *See Flores v. Garland*, 72 F.4th 85, 88 (5th Cir. 2023).

## PA-2025-03: second preference eligibility for NIW petitions

Effective 2025-01-15. Folded into the current USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 6, Part F, Chapter 5 text carried in `policy-manual-eb2-niw.md`. It sharpened the threshold analysis that precedes any waiver question: the occupation underlying the endeavor must itself be a profession, judged by the occupation's general entry requirements rather than the person's own credentials, and bachelor's-plus-five-years experience must be in the specialty.

## What these mean together

Read in sequence, the three alerts describe a narrowing path. PA-2025-03 tightened the threshold that comes before the waiver analysis. PA-2025-16 confirmed a discretionary layer that sits after it. PA-2026-05 removed the assumption that a short filing gets a chance to be cured.

The practical upshot for a self-petitioner is that preparation before filing carries almost all of the weight, and that an honest pre-filing assessment is worth more than an encouraging one.

# What a package review covers, and what it does not

## Contents

- What is reviewed
- What is not reviewed, and why that matters
- What "Filing-Ready" means and does not mean
- If the petition was already filed

## What is reviewed

The petition letter and the supporting evidence supplied in the case: the petitioner profile, the proposed endeavor, the exhibits, the recommendation letters, and the petition draft.

## What is not reviewed, and why that matters

This review does **not** inspect completed USCIS forms, signatures, payment instruments, fee amounts, filing addresses, or submission instructions.

Two rules follow, and both matter to the petitioner:

1. **Do not infer that those checks passed.** Nothing here tells the petitioner their Form I-140 is correctly completed, their fee is right, or their package is going to the right address.
2. **Do not create a finding merely because those materials are absent.** They are out of scope, not missing. A review that flags "no signed form provided" as a deficiency is reporting its own scope as a defect in the case.

For this review, *required initial evidence* means the classification evidence that must support the Form I-140 petition. It does not mean form-preparation or submission mechanics.

This boundary is deliberate. Form completion, fees, and filing logistics are where an unqualified tool does real damage, and they are the part of the process where a licensed attorney or an accredited representative adds the most straightforward value.

## What "Filing-Ready" means and does not mean

`Filing-Ready` means only that the petition and its supporting evidence meet the burden **in this review**, and that the case is ready for the separate final filing checks above.

It does not mean:

- the application is ready to submit,
- the forms, fees, or filing mechanics have been checked,
- approval is likely, or
- any prediction about the outcome.

USCIS adjudication is discretionary, and the waiver is discretionary in nature. A favorable review is an assessment of a record, not a forecast. Never state or imply a probability of approval, in this verdict or anywhere else.

## If the petition was already filed

When the package shows the petition has been filed, a readiness verdict about whether to file is the wrong question, and the review changes shape:

- Assess the package as it stands, and say plainly that the record was already filed.
- Every fix becomes something to raise in a response to a notice, not something to do before filing.
- No fix may rely on a fact that arose after the filing date. See `filing-date-doctrine.md`.

# Legal framework — EB-2 National Interest Waiver

This is the citation set the skill is permitted to draw on. Do not cite cases or authorities outside this list. If you need to reference an AAO pattern not anchored to a named decision, describe the pattern as a tendency in adjudication rather than as a holding.

## Statutory & regulatory

- **EB-2 classification** — INA § 203(b)(2), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(2). Establishes the second preference for members of the professions holding advanced degrees and individuals of exceptional ability.
- **National Interest Waiver** — INA § 203(b)(2)(B)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(2)(B)(i). Permits USCIS to waive the job offer and labor certification requirements when in the national interest.
- **Regulations** — 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(1)–(4). Defines profession, advanced degree, exceptional ability, and the labor-certification-waiver mechanics.
- **Statutory definition of profession** — INA § 101(a)(32).

## Precedent decisions (binding)

- **The three-prong framework** — *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016).
  - Prong 1 (substantial merit + national importance) — pp. 889–90.
  - Prong 2 (well-positioned) — p. 890.
  - Prong 3 (on balance, beneficial to waive) — p. 890.
  - Endeavor as "more specific than the general occupation" — p. 891.
  - National importance ≠ national in scope — p. 889.
- **Preponderance of evidence as the burden** — *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010). Evidence is weighed for relevance, probative value, and credibility, individually and in totality.
- **De novo AAO review** — *Matter of Christo's, Inc.*, 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015).
- **Reserved-issue doctrine (when one prong is dispositive)** — *INS v. Bagamasbad*, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976); *Matter of L-A-C-*, 26 I&N Dec. 516, 526 n.7 (BIA 2015). The AAO may decline to reach later prongs when an earlier prong is dispositive — relevant for understanding why many AAO dismissals do not reach merits on all three prongs.
- **Degree-specialty alignment** — *Matter of Katigbak*, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 46 (Reg. Comm. 1971). The degree or major must be academically appropriate to the profession for which petitioned.

## Federal-court authority on NIW discretion

- **NIW is discretionary; judicial review limited** — *Flores v. Garland*, 72 F.4th 85, 88 (5th Cir. 2023) (joining the Third, Ninth, Eleventh, and D.C. Circuit Courts of Appeals in holding USCIS' decision to grant or deny a national interest waiver is discretionary). See also *Poursina v. USCIS*, 936 F.3d 868 (9th Cir. 2019).

## USCIS Policy Manual

Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 5 (Advanced Degree or Exceptional Ability). Key subsections:

- **5(D)(1) — Eligibility for EB-2 classification.** Advanced degree professional vs. exceptional ability. EB-2 baseline must be established before NIW analysis.
- **5(D)(2) — Eligibility for the NIW (the three prongs).**
- **5(D)(3) — Overview of the three prongs.** Lists the Prong 2 sub-factors USCIS evaluates:
  - Education, skills, knowledge, and record of success in related or similar efforts.
  - Evidence of a detailed proposal or plan.
  - Progress towards achieving the proposed endeavor.
  - Interest or support garnered from potential customers, users, investors, or other relevant entities or persons.
- **5(D)(4) — STEM and critical-and-emerging-technology considerations.** The "strong positive factor" triad for Prong 3: advanced STEM degree (especially Ph.D.) + work in a critical and emerging technology or area important to U.S. competitiveness/national security + well-positioned. All three legs must be present.
- **5(D)(5) — Letters from interested government agencies and quasi-governmental entities.**
- **5(D)(6) — Specific evidentiary considerations for entrepreneurs.** Broad assertions about industry size or general job creation alone will not establish national importance.

## AAO non-precedent decisions referenced for substantive teaching

Non-precedent decisions are persuasive, not binding. They illustrate adjudication patterns. The skill may cite the following decisions as patterns; do not cite other AAO non-precedent IDs.

- **In Re: 37289559 (AAO Mar. 7, 2025)** — Data scientist dismissal. Establishes (or restates) several teaching patterns:
  - National importance focuses on the *specific endeavor*, not the importance of the field generally. At 4.
  - Citation evidence without an independent-citations breakdown is insufficient. At 4.
  - Listing on a grant without PI status is insufficient. At 4.
  - Support letters that describe the petitioner as "remarkable" or "respected" without addressing the specific endeavor's prospective impact are not probative. At 3.
  - The AAO may reserve Prongs 2 and 3 under *Bagamasbad* when Prong 1 is dispositive. At 4.

If you want to teach a pattern that you cannot anchor to one of the above, describe it as an adjudication tendency rather than citing a fabricated decision number.

## What we do not cite

- AAO non-precedent decisions outside the list above. These are not in our curated corpus.
- Internal USCIS memoranda not published in the Policy Manual.
- News reports about USCIS practice. They may inform context but are not authority.
- Other immigration skill suites or community write-ups. They are not authority.
- Specific approved cases from law-firm marketing pages (e.g., Wegreened). They are selection-biased and not authority.

## Glossary

- **EB-2** — Employment-based second preference immigrant visa classification.
- **NIW** — National Interest Waiver. Waives the job offer (and labor certification) requirement attached to EB-2.
- **Labor certification** — DOL process that ordinarily precedes EB-2; tests the U.S. labor market.
- **AAO** — Administrative Appeals Office; reviews USCIS denials.
- **Dhanasar** — 2016 AAO precedent decision that established the three-prong NIW framework.
- **RFE** — Request for Evidence; USCIS asks for more documentation before deciding.
- **NOID** — Notice of Intent to Deny.
- **Preponderance of the evidence** — the legal burden in NIW adjudication; "more likely than not."

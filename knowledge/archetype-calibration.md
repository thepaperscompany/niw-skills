# Archetype calibration

The Dhanasar standard applies identically across profile types. What qualifies as evidence under each prong differs by archetype. This file defines the evidence expectations per archetype so that Prong 2 in particular is evaluated fairly: an industry professional should not be penalized for the absence of publications, and an academic researcher should not be credited for general field importance.

The `applicantArchetype` is either carried over from an upstream endeavors-proposal step or set at evaluation time. State the archetype at the top of the memo with a one-line rationale.

## Academic Researcher

**Includes:** tenure-track or non-tenure-track faculty, postdoctoral scholars, research-staff scientists at universities or national labs, Ph.D. students with substantial independent work.

**Prong 2 evidence expectations (what counts):**

- **Education / specialty.** Terminal degree in the endeavor's specialty. Specialty alignment matters — a CS Ph.D. proposing biomedical-informatics work needs documented specialty bridge.
- **Record of success — citation independence.** Total citations with a breakdown of independent vs. self/collaborator citations. Recent AAO decisions explicitly require this breakdown for citations to carry Prong 2 weight. *See In Re: 37289559* at 4.
- **Record of success — adoption.** Datasets, methods, code, frameworks adopted by other research groups, with named adopters where possible.
- **Record of success — peer service.** Editorial boards, journal reviewing, conference program committees, NSF / NIH panel service. Especially powerful if for venues outside the petitioner's home subfield.
- **Model or plan.** Research agenda with milestones, named collaborators or institutions, funding plan.
- **Progress.** Preprints, accepted papers, talks at major venues, datasets released.
- **Stakeholder interest.** Grants as **PI or Co-PI** (contributor status does not count); citations in policy documents tied to the petitioner's specific work; independent expert letters from researchers at other institutions who adopted or built on the work.

**Red flags specific to this archetype:**

- Citation counts without independence breakdown — weakest possible signal even at high counts.
- Listed on grants without role specified — treat as "contributor" until documented otherwise.
- Recommender pool dominated by advisor, current/recent collaborators, or co-authors.
- No evidence of adoption beyond citation count.
- Endeavor framed as a research-area theme rather than specific projects with named outputs.

## Industry Professional

**Includes:** software engineers, data scientists, ML engineers, applied scientists, product managers, designers, researchers at industry labs (without academic publication track), specialists at consultancies or services firms.

**Prong 2 evidence expectations (what counts):**

- **Education / specialty.** Advanced degree in the endeavor's specialty preferred, but documented specialty expertise (certifications, named-track roles, demonstrated skill at scale) can substitute under EB-2 baseline analysis.
- **Record of success — deployment.** Systems, products, or features the petitioner built or led that are demonstrably in use at scale, with named scale ("serves N users at named company"). Generic claims do not count.
- **Record of success — adoption.** Patents licensed or cited by other inventors; technology adopted by other companies (with named adopters); methods used in industry standards or open-source ecosystems with named users.
- **Record of success — recognition.** Industry-analyst mentions tying specifically to the petitioner's work; named press coverage; invited talks at major industry conferences (RSAC, KubeCon, NeurIPS Industry Track, etc.).
- **Model or plan.** Product/engineering roadmap with named stakeholders; documented technical strategy with named beneficiaries.
- **Progress.** Shipped products with metrics; production deployments with named customers; documented growth in usage or scale.
- **Stakeholder interest.** Independent customer references; partnership agreements; standards-body participation; investor interest in the specific work (for spin-out scenarios).

**Red flags specific to this archetype:**

- Work framed as benefiting the employer ("I built feature X for Google") without broader-implications evidence. The "specific employer" carve-out from *Matter of Dhanasar* at 889–90 applies.
- Patents listed without licensing or citation by others — pending or unlicensed patents alone are weak.
- "Internal impact" only — work that exists entirely within one employer's products.
- Recommender pool dominated by current/former managers at the same employer.
- Generic claims of scale without named context ("widely used," "industry-leading").

**Equivalence note:** Industry professionals do not need publications to clear Prong 2. They need **documented prospective impact beyond the employer**, expressed in the evidence forms appropriate to industry work.

## Founder / Entrepreneur

**Includes:** founders and co-founders of U.S.-based companies, especially those in early-stage technology, biotech, or critical-infrastructure sectors. Includes solo founders, technical co-founders, and CEO-type founders.

**Note on framework:** USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 6 Pt. F Ch. 5(D)(6) provides specific evidentiary considerations for entrepreneurs. The general rule: broad assertions about industry size or job creation will **not** establish national importance. The endeavor must be specific and the evidence must be entity-specific.

**Prong 2 evidence expectations (what counts):**

- **Education / specialty.** Documented expertise in the endeavor area. Founders with non-traditional credentials must show specialty through prior shipped work, technical artifacts, or sector-specific experience.
- **Record of success — company traction.** Revenue with named major customers; ARR/MRR figures; user growth with documented scale; named investor commitments; pilot programs with named partners.
- **Record of success — prior ventures or domain achievements.** Prior exits, prior shipped products at scale, prior technical contributions to the field.
- **Model or plan.** Business plan with financial model; go-to-market strategy; named target customer segment.
- **Progress.** Incorporated entity with documented operations; MVP shipped; pilot customers; letters of intent.
- **Stakeholder interest.** Named investors (VCs, angels, accelerators) with documented commitments; named pilot customers; partnerships with established firms; coverage in industry press tying specifically to the entity.

**Prong 1 considerations (entrepreneur-specific):**

The petitioner's company is **not** automatically Prong 1 evidence. The entity demonstrates Prong 2 (well-positioned). For Prong 1, the **endeavor** must independently show national importance: technology with documented adoption beyond the petitioner's own company; market segment with documented public-interest implications; deployment in sectors of documented national concern.

**Red flags specific to this archetype:**

- Reliance on market size or industry-growth statistics ("the cybersecurity market is $X billion") to argue Prong 1. *See* Policy Manual Vol. 6 Pt. F Ch. 5(D)(6).
- General job-creation assertions ("our company will create N jobs").
- Pre-product or pre-revenue companies with no documented pilot customers or LOIs.
- Founder of a consulting firm in a nationally-important field — explicitly insufficient under the Policy Manual.
- Owner-of-a-business framing where the petitioner's role is essentially employer-of-self without unique contribution to the endeavor.

## Clinician

**Includes:** physicians, dentists, advanced-practice clinicians whose endeavor involves clinical care, clinical research, or public-health practice.

**Prong 2 evidence expectations:**

- Specialty board certification in the relevant area.
- Documented patient-care scale (volume, patient population, region).
- Adoption of clinical protocols developed by the petitioner.
- Service in areas of documented physician shortage or public-health priority — but note that a labor shortage alone does not satisfy Prong 3 (USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 6 Pt. F Ch. 5(D)(3)).
- Hospital privileges, leadership roles, named patient outcomes.
- Independent peer recognition (specialty society leadership, invited lectures).

**Common framing risk:** "Underserved area" arguments alone are weak unless the petitioner's specific clinical work is documented to address an identified national-priority shortage in a way that requires this specific person.

## Artist / Cultural Practitioner

**Includes:** visual artists, performing artists, writers, cultural curators, designers whose endeavor is cultural or artistic in nature. EB-2 NIW for arts is unusual but possible under exceptional-ability prong.

**Prong 2 evidence expectations:**

- Recognition from major institutions in the field (museums, festivals, prize panels).
- Critical reception in major venues (named publications, named critics).
- Works in permanent collections, performances at recognized venues, distribution at scale.
- Cultural impact measurable through documented audience or critical reach.

**Note:** Artists more commonly pursue O-1B (arts) or EB-1A. Confirm with the petitioner whether NIW is the right framework before proceeding with full evaluation.

## How to use this file at evaluation time

1. State the archetype at the top of the memo with one-line rationale.
2. When evaluating Prong 2 sub-factors, calibrate the evidence types against this file. Do not penalize an industry professional for the absence of publications; do not reward an academic for "industry interest" that consists of one talk at one event.
3. When evaluating Prong 1, apply the archetype-specific red flags.
4. When proposing recommender types and information-to-gather items, use the archetype-appropriate evidence categories.
5. Hold every archetype to the same legal standard. Different evidence types, same preponderance bar.

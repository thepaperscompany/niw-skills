# The petition letter

The central document USCIS adjudicates. Everything else in the package exists to support what it argues.

## Contents

- What it has to do
- Structure
- Citing exhibits
- Common failures

## What it has to do

Establish, on the documents filed with it, that the petitioner qualifies for the EB-2 classification and that all three Dhanasar prongs are satisfied by a preponderance of the evidence. It is an argument, not a biography, and it is read by someone looking for the gap.

## Structure

A conventional order, adapted to what the record actually supports:

1. **Introduction**: who the petitioner is, the classification sought, and the proposed endeavor stated in concrete terms.
2. **EB-2 classification**: advanced degree professional, or exceptional ability under the six regulatory criteria. This is the threshold and it comes before any waiver argument.
3. **The proposed endeavor**: what it specifically consists of, its outputs, who benefits beyond the petitioner's own employer, and the pathway by which the benefit travels.
4. **Prong 1**: substantial merit and national importance of that endeavor.
5. **Prong 2**: the petitioner's documented record and why it positions them to advance this endeavor.
6. **Prong 3**: why, on balance, waiving the job offer and labor certification benefits the United States.
7. **Conclusion**.

Sections 4, 5 and 6 carry the weight. Sections 1 and 2 should be short.

## Citing exhibits

- Reference exhibits inline where the claim is made, not in a block at the end of a paragraph.
- Use only exhibit ids appearing verbatim in the manifest.
- Weave citations into the argument. A sentence that asserts something and then lists four exhibits has not shown how any of them establishes it.
- Build cumulatively where several exhibits reinforce one point, and say what each one adds.
- Never cite an exhibit that has not been filed or is not being filed with this petition.

## Common failures

- **Arguing Prong 1 from the field.** Market size, technology lists and the general importance of a problem are framing. The endeavor's own prospective impact has to be shown.
- **Arguing Prong 1 from the petitioner.** Credentials belong to Prong 2.
- **Credentials in place of a record.** A degree from a strong institution does not establish that someone is well positioned. Adoption, citation, funding and third-party interest do.
- **Asserting what no exhibit supports.** Every such sentence is a place an adjudicator can stop.
- **Reciting the CV.** The letter argues; the exhibits document.
- **Sweeping claims of impact** across many national issues, which undermine each other rather than accumulating.
